01 / OUR RESEARCH

Different scales.
A shared set of questions.

From tracing nutrients in the lab to studying the foods Canadians buy and eat, our work explores the connections between diet, metabolism and health.

NUTRITIONAL BIOCHEMISTRY

Sugars & cellular metabolism

How do dietary exposure, sex and physical activity influence the way the body handles fructose and uses energy?

Explore this work +

We use stable isotope tracing and mass spectrometry to follow fructose-derived carbon into downstream metabolites. Complementary cell studies examine how nutrient exposure affects mitochondrial and glycolytic activity.

This work combines animal models and in vitro approaches to understand nutrient handling and metabolic adaptation.

Read the fructose research story →
POPULATION NUTRITION

Protein foods in Canada

What protein foods do Canadians consume, and how do availability, affordability and food quality shape those choices?

Explore this work +

Our Protein Foods Landscape in Canada program brings together dietary surveys, retail food databases and grocery audits. We are developing and validating a protein-food screening tool for population research.

The program examines plant and animal protein foods, consumption patterns, and the demographic and socioeconomic factors associated with food choice.

PUBLIC HEALTH & POLICY

Beverages & food environments

How do beverage prices, marketing and public policy relate to purchasing and consumption?

Explore this work +

With collaborators, we evaluate Newfoundland and Labrador’s sugar-sweetened beverage tax through population surveys, in-store observations, and online grocery price and sales data.

Related work examines beverage attitudes, food insecurity and access to safe drinking water.

Read the research story →
DIETARY FATS

Lipids & nutrient handling

How do the source and structure of dietary fats influence digestion, absorption and metabolism after a meal?

Explore this work +

Our work examines dietary fats, including marine omega-3 sources, and their distribution in the body. Controlled studies and analytical approaches help us investigate postprandial lipid metabolism and differences in metabolic responses.

Read the research story →
LIFESTYLE & HEALTH

Sleep, activity & food choices

How do sleep, physical activity and screen time interact with dietary intake and energy balance?

Explore this work +

Research across adults and young people examines the links between lifestyle behaviours, food choices and energy balance. This complements our laboratory work on the effects of activity and diet on nutrient metabolism.

Read the research story →
MARINE NUTRITION

Aquaculture & bioactives

How can underused marine resources contribute nutritional value to human and animal foods?

Explore this work +

Our green sea urchin research investigates nutritional value in aquaculture by-products and waste streams. Related projects examine how feed and photoperiod influence growth and bioactive compound yield.

Read the research story →

02 / ABOUT SCOTT

A nutritional scientist.
A connected view of health.

Dr. Scott V. Harding is an Associate Professor of Nutritional Biochemistry in the Department of Human Biosciences at Memorial University. His research integrates laboratory studies of nutrient metabolism with population nutrition and policy evaluation.

Scott completed his PhD in Human Nutrition at McGill University, followed by postdoctoral training at the University of Manitoba and Agriculture and Agri-Food Canada. He held an academic appointment at King’s College London before joining Memorial, where he also teaches in the Human Biosciences program.
